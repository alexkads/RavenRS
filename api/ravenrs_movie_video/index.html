§ravenrs-movie-video

Turns a film of ravenrs-movie into an ordinary video file, to publish where the app is not: a video site, a social network, a presentation.

Each picture is drawn by the RavenRS renderer with no window, from the same views the player uses, so the video shows what the app shows: the edited film, the mark of what is being explained, its box, the pointer and the zooms. The sound is the film’s clicks and keys and the narration recorded for each box.

ⓘ
use ravenrs_movie_video::{Video, VideoFormat};

let video = Video::new(movie.clone(), VideoFormat::WIDE).narration(narration.clone());
let report = video.write("demo.mov", |done, all| eprintln!("{done}/{all}"))?;
std::fs::write("cover.png", video.thumbnail(12_000)?)?;
std::fs::write("demo.srt", ravenrs_movie_video::subtitles(&[movie.clone()]))?;
std::fs::write("chapters.txt", ravenrs_movie_video::chapters(&[movie]))?;
FormatSizeLayout
VideoFormat::WIDE1920 × 1080The film fills the picture, boxes over it
VideoFormat::VERTICAL1080 × 1920Headline, film and words under it, up to 3 minutes
VideoFormat::PORTRAIT1080 × 1350The same, for a post in a feed
VideoFormat::SQUARE1080 × 1080The same, square

On a tall screen the film is small; Movie::following brings the camera closer to each spot it explains, so it stays readable on a phone.

The file is QuickTime (.mov) with H.264 pictures and plain 16-bit sound at 48 kHz, which video sites, players and editors read. The pictures are coded by OpenH264, built from its source, so it needs no program installed and works the same on macOS, Linux and Windows. It needs a GPU, as the app does.

subtitles writes what the boxes say as .srt, and chapters writes the chapters as the lines a video description turns into chapters.

It needs Rust 1.89, one version above the rest of RavenRS, because the SIMD crate the H.264 coder uses asks for it.

Functions§

chapters
The chapters of films, one after the other, as lines “0:00 Title” that video sites turn into chapters when pasted in the description. The first one starts at 0:00, which those sites require, even when the film opens on a title card.
subtitles
What the boxes of films, one after the other, say and when, as SubRip subtitles (.srt), which video sites take beside the video.
