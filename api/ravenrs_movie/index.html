§ravenrs-movie

Training videos of a RavenRS app, small enough to ship inside it.

A film here has no pixels. It stores what the app drew, the same rectangles and shaped text the renderer gets, so it stays sharp at any size and zoom, and a minute costs kilobytes instead of megabytes. Built-in fonts are referenced, not copied.

Recording runs the real app from a script. The script clicks by the names screen readers see, so it needs no GPU and no permission from the system, and it runs again when the screens change.

ⓘ
use ravenrs_movie::Recorder;

let mut take = Recorder::new(app, Theme::raven_light(), Size::new(1200.0, 760.0));
take.chapter("A new expense", "How to write down what you spent.");
take.note("Balance", "Your balance", "The sum of all accounts.", 2.0)?;
take.guide("New", "Open the menu", "Click New to begin.")?;
take.click("New")?;
take.type_into("Description", "Groceries")?;
std::fs::write("manual.ravenmovie", take.finish()?.to_bytes())?;

Editing is non-destructive, in the style of a screen-recording editor. A Project is a list of clips and of overlays on tracks: cuts, speed, title cards, callouts, captions, spotlight, hiding what should not show, zoom and pan, fades and a highlight around the pointer. MovieEditor is its timeline, canvas and properties. The edits travel with the film, so there is nothing to render.

Watching is MoviePlayer, with chapters, a position bar and steps that wait for a click:

What the script saysWhat it becomes in the player
chapterAn entry in the list of chapters
noteA mark on the screen whose text appears while it plays, and again on a click
guideThe film stops and waits for a click on that spot
introA chapter, with a box in the middle of the film saying what it teaches
explainA note that lasts as long as its text takes to read or hear

Whatever the film is saying shows in a box next to what it talks about, and a film too small for the box has the words under it.

Hearing needs nothing in the file. Clicks and keys are noted while recording and their sounds are computed, and the system’s own voice reads each box in the language of the labels, so a film weighs the same with sound as without. MoviePlayer has a switch for each. Recording runs with no window, so the click is not captured from a real mouse: it is a click sound made to fit. On Linux the voice needs espeak-ng; without it, the boxes still say it all.

Asking is MovieAssistant. Someone types a question and picks an answer, and the film opens at that moment, with the answers out of the way. A step opens waiting for its click; anything else plays from there.

ⓘ
use ravenrs_movie::{MovieAssistant, MovieLibrary};

let library = MovieLibrary::new().film("Manual", movie);
let assistant = MovieAssistant::new(library).width(960.0);

It searches the words the films hold, the chapters and the steps, ignoring accents and case, and it accepts near forms of a word (“pago” finds “pagar”) and typing slips. There is no model and nothing leaves the machine, so it adds nothing to download and works in the browser. It knows no synonyms: a question with no word in common with the films gets no answer, and it says so.

MovieChat is the same search as a conversation, for a FloatingPanel over the app. Each answer is a card with the moments, and the app decides how to open them. MovieChat::writer lets a local model write the answer about what the search found; finding stays with the search, and a question that finds nothing never reaches the model.

MovieEditor previews the picture only, with no sound.
