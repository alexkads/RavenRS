Declarative views and the UI runtime.

§Writing components

There are three ways to make your own component, from simplest to most powerful:

  1. A function returning impl View, composing existing views. Good for stateless pieces of UI.
  2. A Component: a struct holding its state (State fields, bindings, callbacks) with a body() that composes views. This is the SwiftUI-style building block and covers most needs.
  3. A View implemented directly: full control over layout boxes, painting (including arbitrary Scene drawing through Paint::Custom), hit-testing, focus and click handling via BuildCx. The built-in widgets are written this way, with no private API.

Canvas is the shortcut for custom drawing inside a normal layout.

§Features

  • decode (default): PNG and JPEG decoding for Image::from_bytes.
  • charts (default): Chart, BarChart, AreaChart and Barcode. They draw the same charts and codes as reports, through ravenrs-graphics.

§Runtime

Ui owns the root view and turns it into a Scene: build (views to elements, tracked by a reactive observer), layout (Taffy), paint. It only rebuilds when state the last build read has changed or the interaction state (hover, press, focus) changed, and it never touches the GPU.

§Motion

A view declares how a node moves with BuildCx::motion and the runtime plays it. While only animations run, frames repeat the paint phase alone, so views are not rebuilt. When nothing moves, no frames are requested.

One command of a Toolbar.

One item of a TreeView, with the items under it.

The platform-independent UI runtime: owns the root view, reacts to input and state changes, and produces a Scene for the renderer.

VStack
Arranges children top to bottom.
ValueSlider
A value shown small, as “100% ▾”, that opens a Slider in a popover, for bars with no room for a slider of their own.
VirtualList
A list of any length that builds only the rows that show, with rows of any height. They are laid out and measured as they appear, and what was measured is kept by key, so going back finds them at the right size. It can jump to a row, stay at the end as a chat does, and keep what is on screen still when rows come in above it. With nothing to select, the arrows, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End scroll; with a selection or on_activate, they move between rows, and Enter or Space activates.
WidgetId
Stable identity of a widget across rebuilds, derived from its position in the view tree (or from an explicit key, see BuildCx::keyed_child). Hover, press and focus are remembered per WidgetId.
Wizard
A task in steps: where it stands at the top, one step on the screen, and buttons to go on. A step can refuse to be left, and steps already done can be reopened.
WizardStep
One step of a Wizard.
YearView
The twelve months of a year, with a dot on the days that have something and a click on any of them. As many months go on a line as fit. From the keyboard the year is one Tab stop: arrows move by day and week, Home and End to the ends of the week, Page Up and Page Down by month, and Enter or Space picks.
ZStack
Views on top of each other, the later ones above, in a box as large as the largest.
ZoomControl
Fit, 1:1, the current percentage and the buttons that zoom in and out, for a ZoomView. It works from a toolbar of its own or inside a FloatingToolbar.
ZoomPanPaint
Where drawn content goes, for ZoomPanView::custom and Minimap::custom.
ZoomPanView
Zoom and pan over content: pinch or Cmd/Ctrl with the wheel to zoom, a drag or the wheel to move, a double click between fit and 100%, and the keys +, -, 0, 1 and the arrows (with Shift, further). A thrown drag coasts to a stop, which asks for frames only meanwhile and never happens with reduced motion. The content cannot leave the view.
ZoomView
How a picture is being looked at: how much it is enlarged and which part is in the middle. ZoomPanView, ZoomControl and Minimap share one through a Binding, and each changes what it is responsible for.
