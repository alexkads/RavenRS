pub fn layout(glyphs: Vec<TextGlyph>) -> PageText
Expand description

Reading-order layout. Glyphs are reordered so that glyphs is in reading order, lines are numbered in that order, and word gaps are inferred per line.

Heuristics (documented so they can be tuned and tested; plan/architecture.md §8):

  1. Segments: consecutive glyphs (content order) on one baseline with no column-sized gap. A smaller glyph right next to the previous one (a sub- or superscript) stays in the segment. Segments on the same baseline that nearly touch are merged (text drawn out of order).
  2. Blocks: segments stacked with line-sized gaps and overlapping horizontally.
  3. Block order: repeatedly take the top-most remaining block, preferring a block to its left that overlaps it vertically (so columns read left, then right). A column goes on first (#3658): the nearest block below the last one, overlapping it horizontally and at most 2.5 heights of its last line below it, comes next, unless an unread block sits above it in its own span, sits beside it on its left, or shares its first line (the first lines overlap by at least 30% of the taller one: a table or form row, which reads across, even with labels and values in different sizes or on shifted baselines). Known limit: when a paragraph in the next column starts on the same line as the continuation (text on a baseline grid), it counts as a row and the top-most rule applies, so the column order of #3658 is lost there. Telling prose from a row in that case needs more evidence.
  4. Within a segment glyphs run left to right; runs of right-to-left script are reversed so the text comes out in logical order. A block whose first line sits on the baseline of the line read just before it, to its right and less than [SAME_LINE_GAP] heights away, continues that line after a space (a label and its value, words drawn out of order or in another size). A block of several lines does so only after a one-line block narrower than [SAME_LINE_GAP] heights (a label): beside a wider one, or the last line of a column, it is the next column.
